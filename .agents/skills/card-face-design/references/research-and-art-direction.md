# Research and art direction

## Build evidence that changes the design

Research three separate things: the issuer's identity, the card's construction, and the illustration's meaning. They answer different questions. A brand guide may explain logo colour and clear space without specifying the geometry of an Apple Wallet card. A campaign visual can supply artwork without making its date or promotional text mandatory on the new card.

Start with the user's reference. Isolate the card, correct perspective if necessary, and inspect the corners, inset, lower strip, wordmarks, and secondary devices. Measure normalized positions rather than guessing from a full phone screenshot. Avoid confusing a drop shadow with the card boundary.

For an issuer, seek the official card page, public digital identity assets, relevant brand guidance, and the original identity designer's explanation when typography is unclear. Distinguish the custom logo, supporting font, colour profile, and logo variants for small display sizes. Do not trace a screenshot or approximate lettering before looking for original vectors.

For an illustration project, read the artist credit and concept. Inspect the full main visual, alternate visual, costume/prop sheets, original transparent layers, and relevant official merchandise. Compare source files at natural resolution. A tiny preview with a long pixel dimension created by upscaling is still a tiny source.

Create a source contact sheet. Note what each candidate contributes and why unsuitable alternatives were rejected: crowded composition, clipped silhouette, weak expression at phone size, insufficient resolution, or incompatible lighting. Do not turn exploration into an arbitrary asset quota.

Before composition, be able to explain:

- Which issuer elements carry recognition and which are optional in this brief.
- Which features of the artwork deserve preservation, including their meaning.
- Why the chosen background, material, typography and placement fit that artwork.
- How the alternatives differ in more than colour.

Keep that explanation compact and connect each observation to a design choice.

## Asset provenance

For every used asset, record the source page and direct asset URL separately, artist/publisher, file dimensions, alpha, format/profile, intended role and actual modification. Label new geometry as an interpretation; do not call redrawn orbital lines an extracted official asset.

Use the requested browser/computer workflow when appropriate for acquiring assets. Prefer original downloads to screenshots. If an access or safety control blocks a resource, do not bypass it through a different client, a hidden endpoint, or another domain. Continue with accessible official alternatives and disclose the limitation only if it affects the result.

Do not redistribute raw commercial font files or a folder of downloaded third-party art merely because the finished card is deliverable. Keep project provenance and use authorized assets; do not invent a separate approval ceremony for ordinary authorized design work.

## Example directions, not a fixed recipe

| Principle | Composition and material | What must justify it |
|---|---|---|
| Sculptural | Full silhouette, quiet negative space, pale planes | A detailed, translucent or sculptural source illustration |
| Luxury | Asymmetric vertical identity, dark material, controlled warm light | The art's lighting and a restrained issuer identity |
| Cinematic | Directional figure, atmosphere, luminous hierarchy | The actual scene/concept; not an unrelated stock horizon |
| Travel/editorial | Clear destination or route motif, warm paper, expressive figure | A real travel connection in the illustration project |

Keep the issuer recognisable without reproducing every decoration on its existing card. Do not add fabricated 39 marks, headphones, stamps, or campaign emblems merely because the subject is Miku.

## Worked-source examples

These are useful precedents from the originating project, not permanent asset requirements. Verify availability and suitability when reusing them.

- [MCKL's Amex case study](https://legacy.mckltype.com/custom/american-express): explains the custom wordmarks; it helped distinguish lettering from a substitute font.
- [Amex ANA card page](https://www.americanexpress.com/ja-jp/credit-cards/ana-gold-card/): an example of a card that retains issuer identity within a substantially different composition.
- [Miku's official 16th-birthday site](https://piapro.net/miku16thbd/): main/concept visuals, artist explanation, and wing/costume sheets provided meaningful motifs and scene context.
- [Official Miku Symphony site](https://sp.wmg.jp/mikusymphony/): source for orchestral illustration and lighting references; do not relabel it as a different campaign.
- [Magical Mirai 2024 gallery](https://magicalmirai.com/2024/special_gallery.html): multiple artists and a travel concept supported a distinct alternative to the anniversary artwork.
